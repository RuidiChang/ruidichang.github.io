Multi-agents

Collaborative Adaptation in Multi-agent System at Test-time

Multi-agent language-model systems must do more than divide a problem among several models. Their collective intelligence depends on how well the agents communicate with one another. Training during the task instead changes the collaborative behavior of the agents, like what it shares, what it asks its peers for, and how to folds its contributions into its further reasoning.

Can training at test-time improve a collaborating a syemtem's performance?

  1. 2026

    The Generalization Ridge: Information Flow in Natural Language Generation

    Ruidi Chang, Chunyuan Deng, Hanjie Chen

    COLM 2026

    Models are not monotonically better with depth. InfoRidge traces predictive information across layers: it peaks in the upper-middle layers, a "ridge" where generalization is strongest, then drops in the final layers. Under distribution shift, models down-weight deep layers and rely more on the intermediate ones.

  2. 2026

    PRISM: A Dual View of LLM Reasoning through Semantic Flow and Latent Computation

    Ruidi Chang, Jiawei Zhou, Hanjie Chen

    arXiv preprint

    Reasoning traces are usually studied either as text across steps or as hidden states across layers. PRISM models both at once: an explicit stage labels each step's semantic role (setup, computation, verification, answer) and models how roles flow, and an implicit stage finds latent computational regimes inside each step, linked across steps by a bridge matrix. Failed trajectories are more likely to get trapped in unproductive verification loops and split into distinct modes such as overthinking and premature commitment.

    SAFR: Neuron Redistribution for Interpretability

    Ruidi Chang, Chunyuan Deng, Hanjie Chen

    NAACL 2025 Findings
