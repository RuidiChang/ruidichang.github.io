Current project
Multi-agents

CAT: Collaborative Adaptation at Test-time

Multi-agent language-model systems must do more than divide a problem among several models. Their collective intelligence depends on how well the agents communicate with one another. Training during the task instead changes the collaborative behavior of the agents, like what it shares, what it asks its peers for, and how to folds its contributions into its further reasoning.

Can training during the current task improve a collaborating team's performance beyond providing the same information in context?

  1. Build a target from visible information.With its adapter off, the frozen base model drafts a contribution d, then reviews it under a fixed instruction q, checking claims, omissions and format, to produce the target y.
  2. Update only this agent's adapter.A teacher-forced loss on y (at most 256 supervised positions) updates φᵢ. The base model θ₀ never changes, and adapters are never shared between agents.
  3. Act with the adapted model.The agent generates its message or tool action from the original context with φᵢ⁺, and the action goes to its peers or the environment.

Each agent's information boundary is preserved: targets come only from its visible context, never from uncommunicated peer information, reference answers or future outcomes. Adapters persist across rounds within a task and reset between tasks.

  1. 2026

    The Generalization Ridge: Information Flow in Natural Language Generation

    Ruidi Chang, Chunyuan Deng, Hanjie Chen

    COLM 2026

    Models are not monotonically better with depth. InfoRidge traces predictive information across layers: it peaks in the upper-middle layers, a "ridge" where generalization is strongest, then drops in the final layers. Under distribution shift, models down-weight deep layers and rely more on the intermediate ones.
